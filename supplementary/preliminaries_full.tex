\section{Preliminaries: Turing's Theory of Automata}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

We fix notation drawn directly from
Turing~\cite{turing1936computable},
Rabin and Scott~\cite{rabin1959finite},
and Hopcroft, Motwani, and Ullman~\cite{hopcroft2006automata}.
Nothing in this section is original.  The section exists because
every claim in the sequel is stated in terms of these objects, and
any deviation from their classical definitions would propagate as
ambiguity in our results.

\subsection{Alphabets, strings, and languages}

A finite, non-empty set $\Sigma$ is an \emph{alphabet}.  A finite
sequence $w = a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n$ with $a_i \in \Sigma$ is a
\emph{string}; its length is $|w| = n$; the empty string is
$\varepsilon$ with $|\varepsilon|=0$.  The set of all finite
strings over $\Sigma$ is $\Sigma^*$, and $\Sigma^+ = \Sigma^*
\setminus \{\varepsilon\}$.  A \emph{language} over $\Sigma$ is a
subset $L \subseteq \Sigma^*$.  Concatenation is associative with
identity $\varepsilon$.  We write $L_1 L_2 = \{wv : w\in L_1,
\ v\in L_2\}$ and $L^* = \bigcup_{n\ge 0} L^n$.

\subsection{Grammars and the Chomsky hierarchy}

A \emph{grammar} is a tuple $G = (V, \Sigma, P, S)$ where $V$ is a
finite set of nonterminals, $\Sigma$ is the (terminal) alphabet
disjoint from $V$, $P \subseteq (V \cup \Sigma)^* V (V \cup
\Sigma)^* \times (V \cup \Sigma)^*$ is a finite set of production
rules, and $S \in V$ is the start symbol.  The language $L(G)
\subseteq \Sigma^*$ is the set of terminal strings derivable from
$S$ by repeated application of rules in $P$.

The Chomsky hierarchy stratifies grammars by the form of the
rules in $P$:
\begin{description}
\item[Type-0 (unrestricted).]
$\alpha \to \beta$ for any $\alpha,\beta\in (V\cup\Sigma)^*$ with
$\alpha$ containing at least one nonterminal.  $L(G)$ is
\emph{recursively enumerable}.
\item[Type-1 (context-sensitive).]
$\alpha A \gamma \to \alpha\beta\gamma$ with $|\beta|\ge 1$, plus
optionally $S\to\varepsilon$.  $L(G)$ is \emph{context-sensitive}.
\item[Type-2 (context-free).]
$A \to \beta$ with $A\in V$.  $L(G)$ is \emph{context-free}.
\item[Type-3 (regular).]
$A \to aB$ or $A \to a$ with $A,B\in V,\ a\in\Sigma$ (right-linear,
equivalently left-linear).  $L(G)$ is \emph{regular}.
\end{description}
The strict inclusions
\[
\mathcal{L}_3 \subsetneq \mathcal{L}_2 \subsetneq \mathcal{L}_1
\subsetneq \mathcal{L}_0
\]
hold by elementary
construction~\cite{hopcroft2006automata}.

\subsection{Recognising automata}

To each Chomsky class corresponds a class of recognising machines.
\begin{description}
\item[Finite Automaton (FA, recognises Type-3).]
A tuple $M = (Q, \Sigma, \delta, q_0, F)$ with $Q$ finite,
$\delta: Q \times \Sigma \to Q$ deterministic (or $Q \times
(\Sigma \cup \{\varepsilon\}) \to 2^Q$ nondeterministic),
$q_0\in Q$, $F\subseteq Q$.  Membership decides in $O(|w|)$
time by Rabin and Scott~\cite{rabin1959finite}.
\item[Pushdown Automaton (PDA, recognises Type-2).]
A finite-state controller equipped with an unbounded LIFO stack.
Membership decides in $O(|w|^3)$ time by the standard CYK or
Earley parser~\cite{aho1972theory}.
\item[Linear-Bounded Automaton (LBA, recognises Type-1).]
A nondeterministic Turing machine whose tape head is restricted to
the cells initially occupied by the input.  Membership is
PSPACE-complete in $|w|$~\cite{hopcroft2006automata}.
\item[Turing Machine (TM, recognises Type-0).]
A tuple $M = (Q, \Sigma, \Gamma, \delta, q_0, q_\text{acc},
q_\text{rej})$ with $\Gamma$ the tape alphabet
($\Sigma\subseteq\Gamma$), $\delta: Q\times\Gamma \to Q\times
\Gamma\times\{L,R\}$ a partial function, and the standard
two-way tape semantics of
Turing~\cite{turing1936computable}.  Membership is, in general,
\emph{undecidable}; the universal machine of
Turing~\cite{turing1936computable} establishes that the
recursively enumerable languages are exactly the languages
generated by Type-0 grammars.
\end{description}

\subsection{Recursive enumeration and undecidability}

A language $L\subseteq \Sigma^*$ is \emph{recursively enumerable}
(r.e., or \emph{semi-decidable}) if there exists a Turing machine
$M$ such that, started on the empty input, $M$ prints exactly the
strings of $L$ on its output tape (in some order, possibly with
repetitions).  Equivalently, $L$ is r.e.\ iff there exists a Turing
machine $M_L$ that halts and accepts on input $w$ iff $w\in L$,
and may run forever on $w\notin L$.

A language $L$ is \emph{decidable} (\emph{recursive}) if both $L$
and its complement $\overline{L}$ are r.e.; equivalently, if there
exists a Turing machine that halts on every input and accepts iff
$w\in L$.  The strict inclusion of decidable r.e.\ languages
inside r.e.\ languages is a theorem of
Turing~\cite{turing1936computable}; the undecidability of the
membership problem for arbitrary r.e.\ languages is the
\emph{Halting Problem}, also Turing's.

A celebrated and non-elementary characterisation of the r.e.\ class
is Robinson's:  the r.e.\ subsets of $\mathbb{N}^k$ are exactly the
Diophantine subsets~\cite{robinson1949definability,davis1961decision}.
The corollary --- that there is no algorithm deciding membership
in an arbitrary r.e.\ set defined by Diophantine constraints ---
is the form in which we use the result.  An agent analysed under a Type-0 abstraction therefore inherits,
by the same line of argument, the property that no algorithm decides
membership in the abstract trace language.

\subsection{Idealised versus physically bounded machines}

Every physical computation is bounded: the tape is a finite array
of cells, the time is finite, the energy is finite.  The
\emph{idealisation} we adopt is the standard one of
Hartmanis and Stearns~\cite{hartmanis1965computational}: we
analyse machines whose tape and time are unbounded in the formal
specification, while observing that any concrete execution
witnesses only a finite prefix of the idealised computation.

For a Turing machine $M$, fix a tape bound $b\in\mathbb{N}$ and a
time bound $t\in\mathbb{N}$.  The bounded machine $M[b,t]$ is the
restriction of $M$ to executions in which the tape head visits at
most $b$ cells in at most $t$ steps.  Plainly $M[b,t]$ is a finite
state machine: its state space is bounded by $|Q|\cdot
|\Gamma|^b\cdot b\cdot (t+1)$.  Therefore:

\begin{lemma}[Bounded soundness]
\label{lem:bounded-soundness}
If $M$ accepts $w$ in $\le t$ steps using $\le b$ tape cells, then
$M[b,t]$ accepts $w$.  Equivalently, every trace producible by
the bounded machine $M[b,t]$ is producible by the idealised
machine $M$.
\end{lemma}

Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-soundness} is the formal warrant for the
``idealised Type-0 abstraction'' framing in
Section~\ref{sec:methodology}.  The Goal-Driven agent, as
implemented on the author's laptop, is a very large but finite
LBA.  For the analysis we treat it as an idealised TM; any trace
the bounded machine emits is also emitted by the idealised
machine, and the verification claims we discharge against the
idealised machine apply a fortiori to the bounded one.

\subsection{Closure properties}

We will need the following standard
results~\cite{hopcroft2006automata}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Regular languages are closed under union, intersection,
complement, concatenation, and Kleene star.
\item Context-free languages are closed under union, concatenation,
and Kleene star, but \emph{not} under intersection or complement;
however, the intersection of a context-free language with a
regular language is context-free.
\item Context-sensitive languages are closed under union,
intersection, and concatenation; closure under complement was
established later (Immerman--Szelepcs\'enyi, beyond our citation
discipline; we use only union and intersection in this paper).
\item Recursively enumerable languages are closed under union,
intersection, and concatenation; intersection with a regular
language is r.e.
\end{enumerate}

The closure of r.e.\ under intersection with regular languages is
the closure that drives Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}: the validated
trace language --- the intersection of the Goal-Driven Type-0 abstraction
agent's trace language with the Data-Driven Type-3 verifier's
trace language --- is r.e., but its membership question is
\emph{decided} by the verifier's DFA whenever a trace is emitted
to the disk.  Conformance checking against the verifier specification remains Type-3.
