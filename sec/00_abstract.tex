\begin{abstract}
We develop a trace-language account of agent behaviour and apply it
to a producer--consumer architecture.  A goal-driven generator
proposes traces; a data-driven regular verifier accepts only those
satisfying a specification.  The central result is deliberately
modest: intersecting a generator's trace language with a regular
verifier language preserves the original Chomsky class, so
conformance can be checked in linear time by a DFA even when full
membership remains undecidable.  The verifier is implemented as an
Aho--Corasick keyword automaton compiled from unit-test and schema
checks.  On the \textsc{Spaceship Titanic} task from
\textsc{MLE-bench}, the architecture achieves leaderboard placement
$710/2330$ (top tertile).  A controlled ablation shows the regular
verifier raises mean task score from $35.2\%$ to $57.8\%$ while
eliminating undetected trace faults, at milliseconds versus seconds
and thousands of tokens for an LLM judge.  Agent behaviour can be
represented as a trace language, and a regular verifier can constrain
a more expressive generator while remaining computationally tractable.
\end{abstract}
