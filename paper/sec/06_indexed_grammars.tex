\section{The Pipeline DFA Verifier}
\label{sec:indexed}

This section details the formal mechanism of the Data-Driven
verifier. While early iterations of this work employed an
Aho--Corasick keyword index over unit-test catalogues, the
verifiable constraints of the \textsc{NeuroGolf ONNX} pipeline are
better captured by a deterministic finite automaton (DFA) that
tracks the global state of the task orchestration. This DFA ensures
that the Type-0 Goal-Driven generator produces sequences of
computational operations that are logically sound and structurally
bounded.

\subsection{The Data-Driven verifier as a DFA}

In our empirical setting, the verifier $\mathcal{V}$ is implemented
as a state machine whose state set $Q_\mathcal{V}$ corresponds
directly to the lifecycle phases of an ARC-AGI graph solver
(e.g., \textsf{INIT}, \textsf{ANALYZED}, \textsf{BUILT},
\textsf{OPTIMIZED}, \textsf{V\_TRAIN}, \textsf{V\_TEST},
\textsf{PACKAGED}, \textsf{SUBMITTED}, \textsf{REJECTED},
\textsf{ERROR}).

Transitions between these states are triggered by the operation
symbols ($\Sigma$) emitted by the Goal-Driven sub-agents. For
example, the transition from \textsf{ANALYZED} to \textsf{BUILT}
is triggered by graph-construction operations like
\textsf{BUILD\_ONNX} or \textsf{CONVOLUTION}; the transition from
\textsf{BUILT} to \textsf{OPTIMIZED} is triggered by
transformations such as \textsf{FP16\_SURGERY} or
\textsf{CAST\_COLLAPSE}.

\begin{proposition}[Verifier as DFA]
\label{prop:verifier-dfa}
The Data-Driven verifier $\mathcal{V}$ is realisable as a
deterministic finite automaton over $\Sigma$ with state set
$Q_\mathcal{V}$.  For any candidate trace
$\tau\in\Sigma^*$, the verdict $h_\mathcal{V}(\tau)\in\{
\mathsf{accept},\mathsf{reject}\}$ is computed in $O(|\tau|)$
time.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Construct the DFA over the pipeline states; declare its accepting
states $F_\mathcal{V}$ to be those indicating successful completion
(e.g., \textsf{SUBMITTED} or \textsf{PACKAGED}). The size of the
state machine is $O(1)$ relative to the input, bounded by the fixed
number of pipeline states and valid operation transitions. The
linear-time membership decision $O(|\tau|)$ is a fundamental
property of all deterministic finite
automata~\cite{rabin1959finite}.\qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why a DFA rather than a generic LLM judge}

A generic LLM-as-judge could, in principle, also play the role of
$\mathcal{V}$.  We deliberately chose a rigid DFA for three
reasons:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Class containment.}  An LLM-as-judge is itself a
Type-0 agent.  Composing two Type-0 agents does not lower the
class.  A DFA is Type-3 and lowers the
verification budget to $O(|\tau|)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}.
\item \emph{Auditability.}  The pipeline states and transition
matrix are finite, human-readable, and version-controlled. An
LLM-as-judge offers no such audit surface and suffers from inherent
stochasticity.
\item \emph{Soundness against the Type-0 generator.}  If the
generator is permitted to enumerate arbitrary strings, the
verifier must be unable to be ``talked into'' accepting a
malformed trace (e.g., submitting an un-optimized or
failing graph). A DFA cannot be talked into anything; it
either transitions legally or it rejects.  This is the precise
sense in which the Goal-Driven Type-0 agent is \emph{tamed} by the
Type-3 verifier.
\end{enumerate}

A systematic comparison of the verification cost across all four
Chomsky classes --- including the trade-off between expressiveness
and decidability for Type-2, Type-1, and Type-0 verifiers --- is
given in Section~\ref{sec:verifier-ladder}.  The conclusion of that
analysis is that Type-3 is the principled floor: it is the only class
that preserves both decidability and the $O(n)$ verification budget.

\subsection{The recursive enumeration step, decoded}

We can now describe the recursive enumeration step that expands
the basic \emph{start language} into the highly complex empirical
language recorded in \texttt{experiment\_6/trace\_language.csv}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $\Sigma_0$ be the operation alphabet of the initial
seed templates. $\mathcal{V}_0$ is initialized as the DFA governing
the pipeline lifecycle.
\item Run the Goal-Driven generator $\mathcal{G}_0$ over the
\textsc{NeuroGolf ONNX} input, intersecting every emitted
prefix with $L(\mathcal{V}_0)$ and rejecting prefixes that
drive the DFA into the \textsf{ERROR} or \textsf{REJECTED} states.
\item During execution, $\mathcal{G}_0$ proposes dynamic graph
constructions or repair sequences. The trace lengthens recursively
until it either hits an iteration bound or produces a sequence
that drives the DFA to an accepting state (\textsf{SUBMITTED}).
\item The accumulated trace is written to disk as
\texttt{trace\_language.csv}. Its alphabet $\Sigma_1$ properly
extends $\Sigma_0$; its sub-agent count expands, manifesting
new dynamically assembled operational flows (e.g., the
\texttt{blender} and \texttt{optimizer} components).
\end{enumerate}

The procedure is, in the operational sense, a Type-0 recursive
enumeration: the agent enumerates strings, the verifier filters
them, and the recursion terminates when the enumerated language
satisfies all properties of the data-driven specification.  In
the literal sense of
Robinson~\cite{robinson1949definability,davis1961decision} the
recursion has no general termination guarantee; in our bounded
laptop the recursion is forced to halt by the LBA bound described
in the next section.
