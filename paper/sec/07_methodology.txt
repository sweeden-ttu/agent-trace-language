# Summary

Describes the Goal-Driven/Data-Driven architecture operationally: the two principal agents (G and V), the binding loop, derivation of Type-2/Type-1 sub-agents via projection, Algorithm 1 (recursive enumeration), Hoare-triple obligation per step, and temporal-logic correctness properties. Now includes a forward reference to §10.4 (verifier-expressiveness ladder) explicitly justifying the Type-3 verifier choice as the principled floor.

# Strengths

- Algorithm 1 (RecursivelyEnumerate) is clear and well-structured.
- The forward reference to §10.4 in the binding subsection now makes the Type-3 choice a derived conclusion rather than an assumption.
- The Hoare-triple/LTL content (§7.5–7.6) correctly frames the architecture as "verification-by-construction."
- The trace_language_start.csv and trace_language.csv references are now consistent with actual artefacts.

# Weaknesses

- **Algorithm 1 still does not specify what happens when the LLM produces a malformed continuation.** The recovery path (feed failure back as percept) is described at one line; a concrete encoding of the failure percept is missing.
- **The Hoare-triple claim (§7.5) is not directly implemented.** The DFA verifier checks symbol-sequence membership, not formal pre/post-conditions on a typed state space. The claim is that the DFA transition acts as a mechanical Hoare-triple checker — this is philosophically correct but technically imprecise. No concrete triple is exhibited.
- **The LTL safety/liveness formulas are stated but not model-checked.** The paper says they are "decided by inspection of the model-checker on the orchestrator's CFG" — but no Kripke structure is provided and no model checker was run.
- The projection theorem (Theorem 2) is mathematically trivial; the paper correctly notes this in the proof sketch but the surrounding prose still uses "emerge" language that overstates the novelty.
