\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We discuss six matters: the meaning of ``idealised Type-0'' on a
laptop; the role of the start language; the producer--consumer
restatement and the Type-3 producer subtlety; the verifier-expressiveness
ladder (why Type-3 is the principled sweet spot, not an assumption);
threats to validity; and the relationship between the trace-language
theory and the prior literature on agent architectures.

\subsection{Idealised Type-0 versus realised LBA}

The Goal-Driven producer's trace language is, on paper,
recursively enumerable.  In practice, the producer runs on a
single laptop with a finite tape and a finite execution budget,
supplemented by the High Performance Computing Center at
Texas Tech University for the heavier ablation runs.  The
realised machine is therefore a (very large) linear-bounded
automaton --- strictly Type-1.  We claim the Type-0 framing is
nonetheless the right one for the analysis, on the basis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded-soundness}: every trace that the bounded
machine emits is also a trace of the idealised machine, and the
verification claims we discharge against the idealised machine
apply a fortiori to the bounded one.

The asymmetry matters in only one direction.  Were we to claim a
\emph{lower bound} on the producer's expressive power --- e.g.,
that it can recognise an arbitrary recursively enumerable
language --- the laptop bound would refute the claim.  We make
no such claim.  The Type-0 framing is used \emph{upper}, not
lower:  it is the smallest class within which the producer's
enumeration behaviour fits, and it is the class within which the
undecidability shadow of
Robinson's MRDP theorem~\cite{robinson1949definability,davis1961decision}
applies.  The Type-3 consumer brings the verification problem
back inside the decidable, and the laptop bound brings the
actual execution back inside the finite; both are sound
restrictions.

In Hartmanis--Stearns'
language~\cite{hartmanis1965computational}, the realised machine
inhabits a complexity class strictly inside Type-1.  In
Turing's~\cite{turing1936computable} language, it is a finite
restriction of a universal machine.  Both views are correct;
neither is more correct than the other.

\subsection{Why the start language is necessary}

It would be tempting to dispense with the start language and ask
the Goal-Driven producer to invent its own operation alphabet.
We attempted this in pilot runs and found, predictably, that the
producer's enumeration drifts into pathological corners of
$\Sigma^*$ that the verifier was not designed to cover.  The
start language $L_0$ serves two formal roles:
\begin{enumerate}
\item It fixes the seed alphabet $\Sigma_0$ over which the
verifier's transition table $\delta_\mathcal{V}$ is defined.  Without
$\Sigma_0$, the pipeline DFA cannot be initialised.
\item It provides a finite syntactic skeleton from which the
Type-0 enumeration can extend in a structured way.  The producer
treats $L_0$ as a base case for its recursion, not as a fixed
ceiling.
\end{enumerate}
The start language is therefore the inductive hypothesis of the
recursive enumeration; without it, the recursion has no base.
The empirical CSV \texttt{experiment\_6/trace\_language\_start.csv} is the
artefact form of the inductive hypothesis (7 sub-agents, 9 operation
symbols).  The realised CSV \texttt{experiment\_6/trace\_language.csv}
is the conclusion of one inductive step (11 sub-agents, 17 operation
symbols, 9 novel symbols outside $\Sigma_0$).  More inductive steps (one per task instance)
would produce more emergent columns; the architecture
generalises straightforwardly to the full \textsc{MLE-bench}
suite.

\subsection{Producer--consumer, and the Type-3 producer subtlety}
\label{sec:type3-producer}

The Koohestani--Li--Podkopaev--Izadi
question~\cite{koohestani2025automata} asks \emph{what class is
this agent?}\ and stops there.  The producer--consumer
question of Dijkstra~\cite{dijkstra1968cooperating} asks
\emph{how do classes \textbf{interact}?}\ --- which is the
question this paper answers.  In the canonical setting, the
producer runs the harder enumeration and the consumer runs the
cheaper check.  In our binding, the producer is Type-0 and the
consumer is Type-3, and the verification budget falls to $O(n)$
per candidate trace by Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}.

A natural question follows.  \emph{May a Type-3 agent itself act
as a producer?}  The answer is yes, with one essential condition.
Concretely, in our system the pipeline DFA's transition table
$\delta_\mathcal{V}$ can be extended when a new operation symbol
is deemed valid (e.g., a new graph-construction operation is added
to the ONNX spec).  Adding a row to the transition table is
a backward inference from the data side onto the tape: the Type-3
consumer becomes, locally, a producer that constrains the
principal producer's future emissions.

The condition is decidability.  The Type-3 producer's loop must
terminate in a decidable verdict, otherwise the classical
halting problem of Turing~\cite{turing1936computable}
re-asserts itself on the consumer side and the architecture
loses its verification budget.  Concretely: if the
transition-table update rule is itself Type-0 (e.g., ``add a
transition if the LLM judges it valid''), the rule's trace
language is undecidable, and there exist runs in which the
transition table never stabilises, the verifier never accepts,
and the system never terminates.

The escape is to make the transition-table update rule itself
Type-3 --- i.e., a finite catalogue of admissible transition
extensions --- or, where this is infeasible, to insert a final
\emph{human} verification step that closes the loop.  In our
realised system, both escapes are used.  The DFA transitions
are a finite, version-controlled table; the leaderboard
submission is reviewed by the first author before upload.
The system is therefore a Type-3-bounded Type-0 producer in the
inner loop, and a human-in-the-loop closure on the outer loop.
Both are sound; neither, alone, would be.

This is the operational meaning of ``yes, with software
verification and validation techniques inferred into the
Data-Driven agent.''  The V\&V techniques are precisely the
machinery of Hoare~\cite{hoare1969axiomatic},
Dijkstra~\cite{dijkstra1975guarded}, and
Pnueli~\cite{pnueli1977temporal}
applied at the regular level of the pipeline DFA consumer.
They are not a metaphor; they are the 83-transition table, the
13-state DFA $\mathcal{V}$, and the safety invariant
$G(\mathit{emit} \rightarrow \mathit{DFA\_advances})$.

\subsection{The verifier-expressiveness ladder}
\label{sec:verifier-ladder}

Our choice of a Type-3 DFA as the verifier $\mathcal{V}$ is not
arbitrary: it is the conclusion of a systematic trade-off across all
four Chomsky classes.  We present that trade-off explicitly, since
it pre-empts the natural question of whether a more expressive
verifier would yield a stronger architecture.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{The verifier-expressiveness ladder.  Each row shows what is
gained and lost by placing $\mathcal{V}$ in that Chomsky class.
The column ``Budget'' is the per-candidate-trace membership cost.}
\label{tab:verifier-ladder}
\begin{tabular}{l|l|l|l|l}
\hline
\textbf{Class} & \textbf{Recogniser} & \textbf{Budget} &
  \textbf{Properties checkable} & \textbf{Termination} \\ \hline
Type-3 & DFA & $O(n)$, $O(1)$ sp.
  & stage ordering, bounded counters
  & always; decidable \\
Type-2 & (D)PDA & $O(n^3)$
  & matched acquire/release, nesting
  & decidable \\
Type-1 & LBA & PSPACE
  & counting deps, cross-serial
  & decidable, impractical \\
Type-0 & TM & undecidable
  & arbitrary semantic checks
  & may not halt \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Why not Type-2?}
A pushdown verifier could, in principle, check whether
\textsf{BUILD\_ONNX} and \textsf{VERIFY\_TRAIN} invocations are
\emph{properly nested}: every build that opens a sub-graph must be
closed before verification.  The pipeline DFA already enforces
this structurally via the state sequence
$\textsf{ANALYZED}\to\textsf{BUILT}\to\textsf{V\_TRAIN}$,
making the stack discipline redundant.  The budget climbs from
$O(n)$ to $O(n^3)$ (Earley parsing~\cite{aho1972theory}).
For the 39-step NeuroGolf trace, this is harmless; for traces of
$10^4$ steps the cost is $10^{12}$ operations.  We adopt Type-2 as
an analysed alternative, not a deployed one; it is the right
upgrade if deeply nested build/teardown cycles emerge in future
tasks.

\paragraph{Why not Type-1?}
A linear-bounded verifier could enforce counting constraints such
as ``\textsf{FP16\_SURGERY} applied to every node built by
\textsf{BUILD\_ONNX}.''  The PSPACE membership cost would make
the verification budget collapse: Corollary~\ref{cor:budget} no
longer holds, because the verifier itself becomes the bottleneck.
The architectural value of Type-3 is precisely that
the $O(n)$ DFA cost is dominated by the $O(T_{\text{LLM}})$ cost
of the generator, leaving the budget structurally intact.

\paragraph{Why not Type-0?}
A Type-0 verifier that may not halt is not a verifier at all:
a checker that can diverge provides no soundness guarantee.
Section~\ref{sec:type3-producer} already established this: if the
verifier's update rule is Type-0, the halting problem re-enters
through the consumer side and the architecture loses its
verification budget entirely.  This is the sense in which Type-3
is not merely the cheapest choice but the \emph{principled floor}:
any weaker class (Type-3, Type-2, Type-1) is a DFA-amenable
extension; any stronger class (Type-0) dissolves the soundness
guarantee.

\paragraph{Summary.}
Type-3 is the sweet spot: it provides $O(n)$ decidable membership,
supports all structural properties required by the NeuroGolf
pipeline lifecycle, and preserves the verification budget of
Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}.  Type-2 is the defensible first
upgrade when nesting depth matters.  Type-1 and Type-0 are not
defensible as verifiers in any architecture that requires a
soundness guarantee.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
The trace-language identity criterion (Section~\ref{sec:trace})
is a strong claim: an agent is \emph{nothing} but its trace
language.  The criterion is well-defined --- two agents are
identified iff $L(\mathcal{A}_1) = L(\mathcal{A}_2)$ --- but it
does discard information about, e.g., the LLM's chain-of-thought
or the planner's intermediate JSON.  We accept the discard
because the discarded objects are not directly verifiable; only
the trace is, and only the trace is what we admit as evidence.

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
The Goal-Driven producer's LLM core is stochastic at non-zero
temperature.  We run with temperature~=~0.0 to maximise
reproducibility, but different prompt paths under identical settings
can still produce trace variations.  The class signature
(Table~\ref{tab:class-signature}) covers the structural footprints
that were consistent across our development runs; the leaderboard
placement figures reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} reflect
the best verified submission.  All code for re-runs is released
with the paper.

\paragraph{External validity.}
The architecture has been instantiated on two tasks:
\textsc{NeuroGolf ONNX ARC-AGI} (the primary domain with a
formally verifiable DFA) and \textsc{Spaceship Titanic}
(secondary, keyword-based verifier).  We expect the
trace-language account to generalise to other structured
enumeration tasks where a finite lifecycle of operations can be
encoded as a DFA.  We do not claim generalisation to tasks
where no finite DFA captures the correctness criterion; agents
operating on images, text, or open-ended code may require an
expanded verifier formalism.

\paragraph{Coverage of the producer--consumer escape.}
The discussion in Section~\ref{sec:type3-producer} relies on
either (a) a regular keyword-update rule, or (b) a human in the
loop.  In our experiments both held; we do not claim a general
solution when neither holds.  The recursion-without-decidability
case is exactly where the halting problem returns and the
architecture provides no warranty.

\subsection{Implications for the field}

Two implications follow.  First, the formal-language framing of
agents need not be metaphorical.  When done at the level of
narrowness of this paper, it produces concrete bounds on
verification cost (Theorem~\ref{thm:closure},
Corollary~\ref{cor:budget}) and operational architectures (one
Type-0 producer, one Type-3 consumer) that are competitive with
the contemporary state of the art.

Second, the multiplication of agent ``roles'' in contemporary
literature --- planner, executor, critic, verifier, summariser
--- is, at least in our case, an emergent phenomenon, not an
architectural choice.  The architect designs two agents; the
binding produces five more.  This is consistent with
McCarthy's~\cite{mccarthy1960lisp} programs-as-data and
Newell--Simon's~\cite{newell1976physical} symbol-system
hypothesis: roles are projections, not primitives.  We
conjecture, but do not here prove, that other contemporary agent
architectures admit a similar derivation: a small number of
principal agents binds a verifier, and the named roles are
projections of the verified trace.

\subsection{What we did not do}

We did not measure the LLM's chain-of-thought.  We did not
compare backbone families.  We did not run a full ablation over
the DFA transition table.  We did not formally prove that the
realised trace is the unique fixed point of the binding.  We
did not provide formal pumping-lemma proofs for the Chomsky
classification of derived sub-agents.  Each is a defensible
piece of follow-up work; none is required for the present claim,
which is that a narrow theory of trace languages, derived from
Turing's automata theory and bound by a Type-3 pipeline DFA,
suffices to produce a competitive result on contemporary
benchmarks while providing a formally grounded account of
why that result holds.
