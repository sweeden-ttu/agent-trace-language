\begin{abstract}
Koohestani, Li, Podkopaev, and Izadi recently asked, ``Are agents
just automata?''~\cite{koohestani2025automata} and answered in
the affirmative by establishing a memory-architecture equivalence
between agentic AI classes and the levels of the Chomsky
hierarchy.  We accept their answer and pose the more useful
question: \emph{can the Chomsky type of one agent be used to
\textbf{bound} the Chomsky type of another, in the manner of the
classical producer--consumer problem?}  This paper answers
\emph{yes}.

We give a self-contained formal theory.  An agent is identified with
its \emph{trace language}: the set of finite strings of effectful
operations the agent emits across the input domain.  Within this
theory, we define a Goal-Driven agent whose trace language is, in
the idealised sense of Turing~\cite{turing1936computable}, recursively
enumerable (Type-0), and a Data-Driven verifier whose trace language
is regular (Type-3).  We emphasise that classifying a physical
LLM-in-a-loop as Type-0 is a formal upper-bound idealisation;
physical LLMs have finite context windows and therefore realise a
linear-bounded automaton.  We prove the bounded machine is a sound
idealisation: every trace it produces is also producible by the
idealised Type-0 machine (Lemma~1).

The central architectural result is that the verified composition
$L(\mathcal{G}\,\Vert\,\mathcal{V}) = L(\mathcal{G}) \cap L(\mathcal{V})$
lies in the same Chomsky class as $\mathcal{G}$ (Theorem~1, a known
closure property of the hierarchy which we here apply as an
architectural principle) and its membership is decidable in
$O(|\tau|)$ time and $O(1)$ space per candidate trace.  The Type-2
(pushdown) and Type-1 (linear-bounded) sub-agents emerge as canonical
projections of the verified trace language rather than as
independently designed components.

We instantiate the theory on the \textsc{NeuroGolf ONNX ARC-AGI}
competition.  The realised pipeline DFA verifier has 13~states and
83~transitions; the empirically measured decision time is
$0.684\,\mu$s per operation symbol (over $10^4$~independent calls),
confirming the $O(1)$-per-step regular-language claim.  The
start language has 7~sub-agents over a 9-symbol alphabet; the
recursively enumerated trace (\texttt{experiment\_6/trace\_language.csv})
expands to 11~sub-agents over a 17-symbol alphabet, with 9~novel
operation symbols absent from the seed.  The architecture achieves
a public score of $5793.14$ on the NeuroGolf benchmark and a
placement of $710/2330$ on the \textsc{Spaceship Titanic} task from
\textsc{MLE-bench}.  Reproducible code, the start-language CSV,
the realised trace CSV, and the DFA verifier are released with this
paper.
\end{abstract}
