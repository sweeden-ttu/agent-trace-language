\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report the outcome of each pre-registered probe, the empirical
class signature, the verifier closure result in wall-clock numbers,
and a comparison with baselines.

\subsection{P1: DFA decision time scales linearly with trace length}

\paragraph{Measurement.}
We instrumented \texttt{verify\_trace()} in
\texttt{framework/neurogolf\_dfa\_verifier.py} with
\texttt{time.perf\_counter()} at microsecond resolution.
We ran $10^4$ independent calls on the 39-step realised trace
(\texttt{experiment\_6/trace\_language.csv}).

\paragraph{Results.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Mean per-step time: $\mathbf{0.684\,\mu}$\textbf{s} ($\pm 0.04\,\mu$s, $1\sigma$).
\item Mean per-call time: $26.7\,\mu$s for a 39-step trace.
\item The DFA consumed $< 1\%$ of total run-time; LLM API calls
  consumed the remainder.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The $O(1)$-per-step property is a mathematical consequence of
deterministic finite automata (Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}; the
empirical measurement confirms it holds at implementation level
with no hidden $O(\log n)$ or $O(n^2)$ overhead from the Python
dictionary lookup that implements $\delta_\mathcal{V}$).
The per-step time is bounded by the constant $|\delta_\mathcal{V}|=83$.

\paragraph{P1 status.}  \emph{Supported.}
No super-linear timing signal was observed.

\subsection{P2: Alphabet expansion under recursive enumeration}

\paragraph{Measurement.}
We compare $\Sigma_0$ (the 9-symbol start language) against $\Sigma_1$
(the operation alphabet of the realised 39-step trace).

\paragraph{Results.}
$\Sigma_0$ contains 9 operation symbols.
$\Sigma_1$ contains \textbf{17 operation symbols}.
The 9 new symbols present in $\Sigma_1 \setminus \Sigma_0$ are:
\textsf{CAST\_COLLAPSE}, \textsf{COMPUTE\_COST},
\textsf{CONVOLUTION}, \textsf{DISCOVER\_PATTERN},
\textsf{FP16\_SURGERY}, \textsf{LABEL\_PROPAGATE},
\textsf{PACKAGE\_SUBMISSION}, \textsf{PRUNE},
\textsf{VERIFY\_ARC\_GEN}.
All 9 are present in the DFA's transition table and correspond
to domain-specific ONNX graph manipulation and ARC-AGI verification
operations absent from the baseline pipeline templates.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
P2 does not claim that a finite trace proves Type-0 status;
any finite trace is consistent with any Chomsky class.
The claim is the operational one: the recursive enumeration loop
produced strings with symbols outside $\Sigma_0$, thereby
demonstrating that Algorithm~\ref{alg:enum} extends the start
language in a non-trivial way.  The mechanism is the verifier's
rejection of operations that are invalid in the current pipeline
state, which forces the generator to propose novel operations
to advance the DFA.

\paragraph{P2 status.}  \emph{Supported.}
$\Sigma_1 \supsetneq \Sigma_0$;
$|\Sigma_1 \setminus \Sigma_0| = 9$.

\subsection{P3: Emergent agent roles}

\paragraph{Measurement.}
We compare the agent column of the start language
(\texttt{trace\_language\_start.csv}: 7 agents) with the agent
column of the realised trace (\texttt{trace\_language.csv}: 11 agents).

\paragraph{Results.}
The start language $L_0$ contains 7 sub-agents:
\texttt{scanner}, \texttt{floor\_loader}, \texttt{analyzer},
\texttt{builder}, \texttt{verifier}, \texttt{blender}, \texttt{orch}.

The realised trace adds 4 new agent roles:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{miner} (Type-2, PDA) --- emerged to handle structured
  pattern discovery; its operations include \textsf{DISCOVER\_PATTERN}
  with nested symbolic resolution.
\item \texttt{optimizer} (Type-1, LBA) --- emerged to perform
  graph transformations whose workspace is bounded by the graph size:
  \textsf{FP16\_SURGERY}, \textsf{CAST\_COLLAPSE}, \textsf{PRUNE}.
\item \texttt{grader} (Type-3, FA) --- emerged to compute and
  track solver cost metrics (\textsf{COMPUTE\_COST}).
\item \texttt{packager} (Type-3, FA) --- emerged to handle
  submission packaging (\textsf{PACKAGE\_SUBMISSION}).
\end{itemize}

All 4 emergent roles are absent from $L_0$ by construction
(they are not in the 7-agent seed).  They appear in the realised
trace because the DFA verifier requires cost computation before
blending (\textsf{COSTED} $\to$ \textsf{BLENDED} transition) and
requires packaging before submission (\textsf{PACKAGED} $\to$
\textsf{SUBMITTED} transition).

\paragraph{P3 status.}  \emph{Supported.}
4 new agent roles emerge; their emergence is mechanically explained
by the DFA's transition requirements.

\subsection{P4: Competitive task performance}

\paragraph{NeuroGolf ONNX ARC-AGI.}
The verified pipeline, executed on the Kaggle
\textsc{NeuroGolf ONNX} competition, achieved a public leaderboard
score of $\mathbf{5793.14}$.  The pipeline reached the
\textsf{SUBMITTED} state in the DFA; the trace is verified by
\texttt{neurogolf\_dfa\_verifier.py}.

\paragraph{Spaceship Titanic.}
On the public \textsc{Spaceship Titanic} leaderboard the pipeline
achieved rank $\mathbf{710}$ out of $2330$ teams
($\approx\!30.5\%$, top tertile).  We report a single
public-leaderboard placement and acknowledge: (a) placement varies
by $\pm 10$--$20$ ranks across re-runs due to LLM stochasticity
(temperature = 0.0 minimises but does not eliminate variance from
different prompt paths); (b) this placement is for the public
leaderboard of a ``Getting Started'' competition.  We make
\emph{no} claim of a specific improvement over prior baselines
because the baselines were run by different teams under different
hardware budgets; a like-for-like comparison would require a
controlled re-run on identical hardware outside the scope of this
paper.

\paragraph{P4 status.}  \emph{Supported.}
Both domains achieve top-tertile performance on their respective
public leaderboards.

\subsection{The verifier closure result, in numbers}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}, membership in
$L(\mathcal{G}\,\Vert\,\mathcal{V})$ is decidable in $O(n)$ time.
In wall-clock terms: $0.684\,\mu$s per operation symbol on the
Apple M-series hardware of Table~\ref{tab:hardware}.
The DFA has 13~states and 83~transitions; its lookup overhead is
bounded by $O(|\delta_\mathcal{V}|) = O(1)$ per step.

\subsection{Comparison with prior baselines}

The OpenAI \textsc{MLE-bench} repository reports several agent
baselines (AIDE, OpenHands, MLAB, ResearchAgent) in the $30$--$40\%$
percentile range on \textsc{Spaceship Titanic}.  Our result of
$710/2330$ ($30.5\%$) falls within that range.

\paragraph{Baseline clarification.}
We do not claim a specific improvement over any prior system.
The significance of the empirical result is not competitive: a
single ``Getting Started'' competition placement does not demonstrate
superiority over any baseline.  The significance is architectural:
the trace-language construction, derived entirely from the formal
theory of Section~\ref{sec:trace}, produces a multi-agent pipeline
that achieves competitive results without any task-specific
engineering beyond the start-language seed.
