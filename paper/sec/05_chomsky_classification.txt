# Summary

Classifies the empirical sub-agents from `experiment_6/trace_language.csv` by Chomsky type: Type-3 (scanner, floor_loader, grader, packager, verifier), Type-2 (analyzer, miner), Type-1 (optimizer), Type-0/idealised (builder, blender). States the formal binding G‖V and the minimality claim. Now references the verifier-expressiveness ladder in §10.4 and explicitly qualifies Type-0 as an idealised upper bound.

# Strengths

- The summary table (Table 1) is clear and covers all 11 sub-agents in the actual CSV (not the obsolete 13-agent description).
- The preamble now correctly names the NeuroGolf ONNX ARC-AGI domain and cites `experiment_6/trace_language.csv`.
- Type-0 is now qualified as an idealised upper bound via Lemma 1 (bounded soundness), removing the strongest version of the Type-0 overclaim.
- The forward reference to §10.4 (verifier-expressiveness ladder) converts the Type-3 verifier from an unjustified assumption into a derived conclusion.
- The minimality slogan is now grounded: the ladder in §10.4 shows Type-3 is the principled floor, not an arbitrary choice.
- The preamble explicitly states classifications are by structural inspection, not pumping-lemma proofs.

# Weaknesses

- **The classification is still post-hoc and structural.** No pumping-lemma or Myhill-Nerode argument is provided for any sub-agent. The preamble now admits this, but the claims in the body text still read as assertions.
- The Type-2 claim for `analyzer` and `miner` relies on "nested graph-building structures that naturally map to context-free productions." This analogy is adequate for a conference paper but would not survive journal review.
- The Type-1 claim for `optimizer` (LBA hallmark: trace bounded by constant multiple of input graph size) is the strongest individual claim. It could be strengthened by measuring actual trace-length growth against input graph size in a scaling experiment.
- Table 1 includes `orch` as Type-3 (FA), which is plausible but never argued.
