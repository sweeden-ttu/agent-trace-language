A Trace-Language Theory of Agents: Goal-Driven Type-0 Producers Bounded by Data-Driven Type-3 Consumers

Scott Weeden Department of Computer Science Texas Tech University Lubbock, Texas, USA

sweeden@ttu.edu

Abstract -Koohestani, Li, Podkopaev, and Izadi recently asked, 'Are agents just automata?' [1] and answered in the affirmative by establishing a memory-architecture equivalence between agentic AI classes and the levels of the Chomsky hierarchy. We accept their answer and pose the more useful question: can the Chomsky type of one agent be used to bound the Chomsky type of another, in the manner of the classical producer-consumer problem? This paper answers yes .

We give a self-contained formal theory. An agent is identified with its trace language : the set of finite strings of effectful operations the agent emits across the input domain. Within this theory, we define a Goal-Driven agent whose trace language is, in the idealised sense of Turing [2], recursively enumerable (Type0), and a Data-Driven verifier whose trace language is regular (Type-3). We emphasise that classifying a physical LLM-in-aloop as Type-0 is a formal upper-bound idealisation; physical LLMs have finite context windows and therefore realise a linearbounded automaton. We prove the bounded machine is a sound idealisation: every trace it produces is also producible by the idealised Type-0 machine (Lemma 1).

The central architectural result is that the verified composition L ( G ∥ V ) = L ( G ) ∩ L ( V ) lies in the same Chomsky class as G (Theorem 1, a known closure property of the hierarchy which we here apply as an architectural principle) and its membership is decidable in O ( | τ | ) time and O (1) space per candidate trace. The Type-2 (pushdown) and Type-1 (linear-bounded) sub-agents emerge as canonical projections of the verified trace language rather than as independently designed components.

We instantiate the theory on the NEUROGOLF ONNX ARCAGI competition. The realised pipeline DFA verifier has 13 states and 83 transitions; the empirically measured decision time is 0 . 684 µ s per operation symbol (over 10 4 independent calls), confirming the O (1) -per-step regular-language claim. The start language has 7 sub-agents over a 9-symbol alphabet; the recursively enumerated trace ( experiment_6/trace_language.csv ) expands to 11 sub-agents over a 17-symbol alphabet, with 9 novel operation symbols absent from the seed. The architecture achieves a public score of 5793 . 14 on the NeuroGolf benchmark and a placement of 710 / 2330 on the SPACESHIP TITANIC task from MLE-BENCH. Reproducible code, the start-language CSV, the realised trace CSV, and the DFA verifier are released with this paper.

Index Terms -Agentic systems, trace language, Chomsky hierarchy, Turing machines, finite automata, recursive enumeration, producer-consumer, formal verification, linear-bounded automata, machine-learning engineering benchmarks.

I. INTRODUCTION

T HE contemporary literature on autonomous software agents is rich in architectural diagrams but short on formal definitions. An agent is variously described as 'a system that perceives and acts', 'a planner that calls tools', or 'an LLM in a loop', and each definition is stated at a level of abstraction that admits neither a decision procedure nor a complexity bound. The consequence is that two agents with radically different computational power and reasoning capabilities are routinely compared as if they belonged to a single class, and verification claims about agentic systems are correspondingly difficult to falsify.

We are not the first to ask whether the Chomsky hierarchy is the right scaffold for thinking about this problem. In particular, Koohestani et al. pose the question Are agents just automata? [1] and answer affirmatively, establishing a memory-based equivalence between simple-reflex agents and finite automata, hierarchical task-decomposition agents and pushdown automata, and unrestricted-memory agents and thus Turing machines [2].

Although the answer provided by Koohestani et al. is generally acceptable, the question they pose is the preliminary one that requires more profound and detailed thinking. The question that does practical work is operational: given that agents are automata, how can the Chomsky type of one agent be used to bound the Chomsky type of another? In fact, this is known as the producer-consumer problem of Dijkstra [3] restated in the language of formal grammars. The present paper offers a formal methodology that answers the posed question in the affirmative, with formal verification techniques inferred into the consumer side and the idealised theoretical apparatus of automata built into the producer side.

A. Why a narrow definition

We deliberately keep the definition of an agent narrow, taking only what is forced upon us by the theory of automata Turing introduced in 1936 [2] and the language hierarchy Chomsky introduced in 1956-59 [4], [5]. We define an agent as 'an effectful machine that, when started on an admissible input, emits a finite string of operation symbols and then halts (or fails to halt)' . The collection of all such strings, taken across the input domain, is the agent's trace language . Two agents with the same trace language are, by stipulation, the same agent. Whatever cannot be read off from the trace language is, for the purposes of this paper, not treated as an agent property.

The motivation for keeping the definition narrow is twofold. First, it gives a direct line from the agent to the Chomsky hierarchy and to the recognising automata. An agent whose trace language is regular (Type-3) is realisable by a finitestate controller and admits decidable verification in linear time [6]. An agent whose trace language is context-free (Type2) is realisable by a pushdown controller and admits O ( n 3 ) membership [7]. An agent whose trace language is recursively enumerable (Type-0) is, in the worst case, undecidable to verify - a fact whose modern statement we inherit from Robinson's resolution of Hilbert's tenth problem [8], [9], which established that recursively enumerable sets are exactly the Diophantine sets and that the membership problem for them admits no general algorithm.

Second, the narrow definition gives an empirical handle. If the agent is its trace language, then the trace language can be recorded, stored, and compared; experiments produce strings, and those strings can be classified by the smallest grammar class that generates them. This places the verification and validation of agentic systems on the same footing as the verification and validation of compilers, parsers, and protocol implementations [10], [11] - domains in which the formallanguage framing has, for half a century, been the foundation of decidable correctness.

B. The Goal-Driven Producer and Data-Driven Consumer

Our central architectural claim is the producer-consumer restatement. There is exactly one Goal-Driven producer agent. Its trace language is Type-0: recursively enumerable and, in general, undecidable. In practice it is realised as a large language model wrapped in a tool-calling loop, and the loop is permitted to enumerate any finite-length sequence of tool invocations from the union of the sub-agents' alphabets. The Goal-Driven producer has no a priori knowledge of what the goal is; it knows only the specification of the input domain and the tool catalogue.

There is exactly one Data-Driven consumer agent, the verifier . Its trace language is Type-3: regular and, by Rabin and Scott's construction [6], decidable in linear time. The verifier is concretely realised as the deterministic finite automaton of an Aho-Corasick keyword index [12] compiled from the competition's empirical fixtures: the unit-test corpus, the submissionvalidity tests, the column-name checks, and the integrity tests. The binding is the entirety of our architectural claim:

A single Goal-Driven Type-0 producer, tamed by a single Data-Driven Type-3 consumer, results in success.

Type-2 and Type-1 sub-agents are not designed; they are ' derived '. They appear as the canonical projections of the recursively enumerated trace onto the parts of the orchestration substrate that admit, respectively, a stack discipline (the back-edges of the planner/developer/reviewer loop, the recursive retry_phase_on_low_score ) and a linearbounded workspace (the deterministic Aho-Corasick scanner and the LBA-style compute_dependency_graph that walks the phase-to-phase dependency relation). We make these projections precise in Section V.

A subtlety: nothing in the framework prevents a Type3 agent from itself acting as a producer, in which case it backward-infers constrained changes onto the tape (e.g., updates to the keyword set K as new task fixtures arrive). Such a Type-3 producer is admissible provided the inference terminates in a decidable verdict. When it does not, the classical halting problem of Turing [2] re-asserts itself on the Type-3 producer's loop and the architecture loses its verification budget. We discuss the engineering consequence in Section X: in the realised system, decidability is enforced by the regular envelope of the verifier and, where the datadriven side begins to drift, by a final human verification and validation step that closes the loop.

C. The Start Language and its Recursive Enumeration

The system is initialised with a single start language L 0 . The start language contains twelve sub-agents and their seed action alphabets - nothing else. The Goal-Driven Type-0 producer, bound by the Data-Driven Type-3 consumer, then recursively enumerates L 0 onto the SPACESHIP TITANIC task instance. The result is the trace language L 1 . L 1 contains thirteen sub-agents. The thirteenth, kaggle_trainer , is the emergent agent: it does not exist in L 0 and is materialised by the act of enumeration , after the verifier first refuses traces that omit the training step.

We claim that this enumeration is the operational meaning of 'Type-0 recursive enumeration' in the agentic setting. Turing's machines enumerate strings; Robinson's theorem connects the enumerated sets to Diophantine arithmetic; our agent enumerates tool-call strings, and the connection between the enumerated strings and the empirical Kaggle competition is mediated by the Aho-Corasick verifier.

D. Idealisation and physical bounding

It is worth being clear about the difference between the theory and the machine that actually ran the experiment. The Goal-Driven agent's trace language is idealised Type-0. The actual execution was bounded by the finite tape and finite RAM of a single laptop and, for the heavier ablation runs, by the High Performance Computing Center at Texas Tech University. The realised machine is, formally, a (very large) linear-bounded automaton - Type-1. The idealisation is sound for our purposes: every trace producible by the realised LBA is also producible by the idealised Turing machine, and the verified trace language is, by closure under intersection at the verifier's Chomsky level (Theorem 2), bounded above by Type-3. The physical computation is therefore far more restricted than the theoretical envelope, and any verification claim we discharge about the idealised system holds a fortiori on the bounded machine. This is the standard idealisation of Hartmanis and Stearns [13], and we adopt it on those terms.

II. RELATED WORK

The literature relevant to this paper falls into five strands. The first asks whether agents are automata, and answers affirmatively in a memory-architecture sense (Koohestani et al. [1]). The second is the producer-consumer framing of cooperating sequential processes (Dijkstra [3]), which is the operational form of the question we actually wish to answer: how can the type of one agent bound another? The third is the classical theory of automata and the Chomsky hierarchy (Turing, Chomsky, Rabin and Scott, Hopcroft and Ullman, Aho). The fourth is software verification and validation (Hoare, Dijkstra, Pnueli, Clarke and Emerson, Lamport). The fifth is the boundary between decidable and undecidable verification fixed by Robinson and the MRDP theorem. We address each in turn.

A. Are agents just automata?

Koohestani, Li, Podkopaev, and Izadi [1] recently posed and answered the title question, establishing a memory-architecture equivalence: simple-reflex agents are finite automata; hierarchical task-decomposition agents are pushdown automata; agents employing read/write memory for reflection are Turing machines. Their paper introduces a right-sizing principle : choose the minimal computational class sufficient for the task.

Two observations are owed to that paper. First, the equivalence they establish is the architectural premise of the present work; without it, the question of how Chomsky classes interact across agents would have no canonical formulation. Second, their paper is preliminary in scope: it classifies agents but does not prescribe a construction in which one agent's class constrains another's class. We supply that construction here. Where they ask Are agents just automata? and answer yes , we ask can the type of one agent bound another? and answer yes, with V&V techniques inferred into a Data-Driven verifier and the idealised theoretical apparatus of automata built into a Goal-Driven producer .

A related strand, Formal-LLM [14], encodes planconstruction constraints as a context-free grammar and guides an LLM-based planner with a pushdown automaton. Our construction is dual: we do not constrain plan generation through a higher-class grammar, we verify plan emission through a lower-class one. Aho-Corasick is the 'less-Turing-completethan-the-LLM' device that earns us the verification budget of Theorem 2.

B. The producer-consumer framing

Dijkstra's cooperating sequential processes [3] fix the producerconsumer formulation: one process produces a sequence of artefacts; another consumes them; correct cooperation requires that the consumer never reads an artefact the producer has not yet produced, and the producer never overwrites an artefact the consumer has not yet consumed. The classical question is: what synchronisation discipline is sufficient? In our setting the question becomes: what Chomskyclass discipline on the consumer is sufficient to render the producer's recursively enumerable output decidable in finite time? The answer, by Theorem 2, is Type-3. Lower would not work (Type-3 is the minimal class that contains the keyword matchers Aho-Corasick implements); higher would work but raise the verification budget unnecessarily and risk the halting problem returning through the consumer's loop.

C. Automata theory and the Chomsky hierarchy

Turing's 1936 paper [2] introduces the abstract machine that bears his name and proves the existence of a universal one. Every formal claim in this paper inherits, at some depth, from that construction. Chomsky's 1956 and 1959 papers [4], [5] fix the hierarchy itself: regular, context-free, context-sensitive, and unrestricted (Type-3 through Type-0). Rabin and Scott [6] fix the formal correspondence between regular languages and finite automata, with the nondeterminism that makes the proofs tractable. Hopcroft, Motwani, and Ullman [7] consolidate the modern treatment with strict inclusions and standard parsing complexities. Hartmanis and Stearns [13] introduce the time and space complexity classes in which our Type-1 / Type-0 distinction ultimately lives, and supply the formal vocabulary for our 'idealised Type-0 versus realised LBA' discussion in Section X.

Aho's 1968 paper [15] introduces indexed grammars as a strict extension of context-free grammars, positioned strictly between Type-2 and Type-1. In our framework the backedge structure of an iterative repair loop - e.g., the retry_phase_on_low_score cycle in Section VII inhabits exactly that zone. We use Aho's indexed grammars as the bridge between the pushdown sub-agent (Type-2) and the linear-bounded sub-agent (Type-1).

The Aho-Corasick paper [12] compiles a finite keyword set into a deterministic finite automaton that recognises the union of the keywords in linear time and constant space. In our framework Aho-Corasick is the canonical example of a Type-3 construction for keyword-based matching. The realised Data-Driven verifier in this paper is instead a pipeline lifecycle DFA compiled from the 13-state transition table of neurogolf_dfa_verifier.py ; it plays the same architectural role as Aho-Corasick but constrains pipeline-stage ordering rather than keyword occurrence.

D. Software verification and validation

Hoare's axiomatic basis [11] and Dijkstra's guardedcommand calculus [16] supply the machinery of Hoare triples { P } C { Q } that we apply to each unit-test step in the AhoCorasick keyword set. The predicates P and Q are the 'before' and 'after' states of a single tool invocation in the trace.

Pnueli's 1977 paper [17] introduces the linear temporal logic in which we phrase the safety and liveness properties of the Goal-Driven loop:

expresses that every plan emitted by the producer is eventually verified or refuted by the consumer. Clarke and Emerson [18] introduce the branching variant we use to reason about the back-edges of the iterative repair loop, and Clarke, Grumberg, and Peled [19] consolidate the model-checking framework that, in principle, decides the Type-3 component of our verifier.

Lamport [20], [21] supplies the ordering on events in the multi-agent execution and the temporal logic of actions; the typesetting of this paper itself uses his L A T E X system [22]. Liskov's abstract data types [23] are the type discipline underlying the TypedDict state objects that flow between subagents.

E. The undecidability boundary: Robinson and MRDP

The boundary between decidable and undecidable verification - which, in our framework, is the boundary between Type-1 and Type-0 agents - is fixed by Hilbert's tenth problem and its resolution. Robinson's 1949 paper [8] and the Davis-Putnam-Robinson follow-up [9] established that the recursively enumerable subsets of N k are exactly the Diophantine subsets, and that the membership problem for an arbitrary recursively enumerable set admits no general algorithm. The Goal-Driven Type-0 producer in our architecture inherits this undecidability directly: in the absence of the Type-3 verifier, no algorithm decides whether a candidate trace lies in the producer's trace language. The verifier is the entire reason the system is decidable in practice.

F. Complexity, learning, and symbolic AI

Cook [24] and Karp [25] fix the NP-completeness frame within which the model-search step of the Goal-Driven producer (the Optuna tuning of LightGBM, the cross-validated stacking ensemble) is, formally, an instance of constrained combinatorial optimisation. Yao [26] supplies the notion of expected complexity used when reporting average trace lengths over the input distribution. Blum [27] fixes the machineindependent measure in which we report the verified trace language's complexity.

The supervised-learning component of the Spaceship Titanic solution is justified by the deep-learning manifesto of LeCun, Bengio, and Hinton [28]; the causal discipline that prevents the verifier from being fooled by spurious correlations is justified by Pearl [29]. McCarthy [30] is the symbolic representation of programs as data - the property that lets a Goal-Driven agent emit a program (a Kaggle notebook) as the terminal element of its trace. Newell and Simon [31] state the physical-symbolsystem hypothesis under which the agent's tool calls are the elementary symbols. We adopt their hypothesis as a working assumption rather than as a contribution.

G. Synthesis

The strands above suffice to support every formal claim in the sequel. In particular, our novelty relative to Koohestani et al. [1] is the producer-consumer construction: where they classify, we bound; where they say which class an agent inhabits, we say which class must consume an agent of class i in order for the system as a whole to remain decidable. The empirical instantiation (Sections VIII-IX) demonstrates the trace-language account on two tasks with a Type-3 pipeline DFA as the verifier. The verifier-expressiveness ladder (Section X-D) establishes that Type-3 is the principled floor: lower than Type-0 (which dissolves soundness) and more budgetpreserving than Type-1 or Type-2. The empirical instantiation is reported as data, not as theory, and stands or falls on its own reproducibility artefacts.

III. PRELIMINARIES: TURING'S THEORY OF AUTOMATA

We fix notation drawn directly from Turing [2], Rabin and Scott [6], and Hopcroft, Motwani, and Ullman [7]. Nothing in this section is original. The section exists because every claim in the sequel is stated in terms of these objects, and any deviation from their classical definitions would propagate as ambiguity in our results.

A. Alphabets, strings, and languages

A finite, non-empty set Σ is an alphabet . A finite sequence w = a 1 a 2 · · · a n with a i ∈ Σ is a string ; its length is | w | = n ; the empty string is ε with | ε | = 0 . The set of all finite strings over Σ is Σ ∗ , and Σ + = Σ ∗ \ { ε } . A language over Σ is a subset L ⊆ Σ ∗ . Concatenation is associative with identity ε . We write L 1 L 2 = { wv : w ∈ L 1 , v ∈ L 2 } and L ∗ = ⋃ n ≥ 0 L n .

B. Grammars and the Chomsky hierarchy

A grammar is a tuple G = ( V, Σ , P, S ) where V is a finite set of nonterminals, Σ is the (terminal) alphabet disjoint from V , P ⊆ ( V ∪ Σ) ∗ V ( V ∪ Σ) ∗ × ( V ∪ Σ) ∗ is a finite set of production rules, and S ∈ V is the start symbol. The language L ( G ) ⊆ Σ ∗ is the set of terminal strings derivable from S by repeated application of rules in P .

The Chomsky hierarchy stratifies grammars by the form of the rules in P :

Type-0 (unrestricted). α → β for any α, β ∈ ( V ∪ Σ) ∗ with α containing at least one nonterminal. L ( G ) is recursively enumerable .

Type-1 (context-sensitive). αAγ → αβγ with | β | ≥ 1 , plus optionally S → ε . L ( G ) is context-sensitive .

Type-2 (context-free). A → β with A ∈ V . L ( G ) is context-free .

Type-3 (regular). A → aB or A → a with A,B ∈ V, a ∈ Σ (rightlinear, equivalently left-linear). L ( G ) is regular .

The strict inclusions

hold by elementary construction [7].

C. Recognising automata

To each Chomsky class corresponds a class of recognising machines.

Finite Automaton (FA, recognises Type-3). A tuple M = ( Q, Σ , δ, q 0 , F ) with Q fi nite, δ : Q × Σ → Q deterministic (or Q × (Σ ∪ { ε } ) → 2 Q nondeterministic), q 0 ∈ Q , F ⊆ Q . Membership decides in O ( | w | ) time by Rabin and Scott [6].

Pushdown Automaton (PDA, recognises Type-2). A finite-state controller equipped with an unbounded LIFO stack. Membership decides in O ( | w | 3 ) time by the standard CYK or Earley parser [10].

  • Linear-Bounded Automaton (LBA, recognises Type-1). A nondeterministic Turing machine whose tape head is restricted to the cells initially occupied by the input. Membership is PSPACE-complete in | w | [7].
  • Turing Machine (TM, recognises Type-0). A tuple M = ( Q, Σ , Γ , δ, q 0 , q acc , q rej ) with Γ the tape alphabet ( Σ ⊆ Γ ), δ : Q × Γ → Q × Γ ×{ L, R } a partial function, and the standard two-way tape semantics of Turing [2]. Membership is, in general, undecidable ; the universal machine of Turing [2] establishes that the recursively enumerable languages are exactly the languages generated by Type-0 grammars.

D. Recursive enumeration and undecidability

A language L ⊆ Σ ∗ is recursively enumerable (r.e., or semi-decidable ) if there exists a Turing machine M such that, started on the empty input, M prints exactly the strings of L on its output tape (in some order, possibly with repetitions). Equivalently, L is r.e. iff there exists a Turing machine M L that halts and accepts on input w iff w ∈ L , and may run forever on w / ∈ L .

A language L is decidable ( recursive ) if both L and its complement L are r.e.; equivalently, if there exists a Turing machine that halts on every input and accepts iff w ∈ L . The strict inclusion of decidable r.e. languages inside r.e. languages is a theorem of Turing [2]; the undecidability of the membership problem for arbitrary r.e. languages is the Halting Problem , also Turing's.

A celebrated and non-elementary characterisation of the r.e. class is Robinson's: the r.e. subsets of N k are exactly the Diophantine subsets [8], [9]. The corollary - that there is no algorithm deciding membership in an arbitrary r.e. set defined by Diophantine constraints - is the form in which we use the result. An idealised Type-0 agent therefore inherits, by the same line of argument, the property that no algorithm decides whether a given trace is in the agent's trace language.

E. Idealised versus physically bounded machines

Every physical computation is bounded: the tape is a finite array of cells, the time is finite, the energy is finite. The idealisation we adopt is the standard one of Hartmanis and Stearns [13]: we analyse machines whose tape and time are unbounded in the formal specification, while observing that any concrete execution witnesses only a finite prefix of the idealised computation.

For a Turing machine M , fix a tape bound b ∈ N and a time bound t ∈ N . The bounded machine M [ b, t ] is the restriction of M to executions in which the tape head visits at most b cells in at most t steps. Plainly M [ b, t ] is a finite state machine: its state space is bounded by | Q | · | Γ | b · b · ( t +1) . Therefore:

Lemma 1 (Bounded soundness) . If M accepts w in ≤ t steps using ≤ b tape cells, then M [ b, t ] accepts w . Equivalently, every trace producible by the bounded machine M [ b, t ] is producible by the idealised machine M .

Lemma 1 is the formal warrant for the 'idealised Type0' framing in Section VII. The Goal-Driven agent, as implemented on the author's laptop, is a very large but finite LBA.

For the analysis we treat it as an idealised TM; any trace the bounded machine emits is also emitted by the idealised machine, and the verification claims we discharge against the idealised machine apply a fortiori to the bounded one.

F. Closure properties

We will need the following standard results [7]:

  1. Regular languages are closed under union, intersection, complement, concatenation, and Kleene star.
  2. Context-free languages are closed under union, concatenation, and Kleene star, but not under intersection or complement; however, the intersection of a context-free language with a regular language is context-free.
  3. Context-sensitive languages are closed under union, intersection, and concatenation; closure under complement was established later (Immerman-Szelepcs´ enyi, beyond our citation discipline; we use only union and intersection in this paper).
  4. Recursively enumerable languages are closed under union, intersection, and concatenation; intersection with a regular language is r.e.

The closure of r.e. under intersection with regular languages is the closure that drives Theorem 2: the verified trace language - the intersection of the Goal-Driven Type-0 agent's trace language with the Data-Driven Type-3 verifier's trace language - is r.e., but its membership question is decided by the verifier's DFA whenever a trace is emitted to the disk. Verification cannot exceed Type-3.

IV. THE TRACE LANGUAGE OF AN AGENT

This section is the formal core of the paper. We define an agent as a tuple of seven objects, three of which are derivative. We then define the agent's trace language and prove that the definition satisfies the Chomsky hierarchy verbatim. The construction is austere and self-contained; nothing beyond Section III is assumed.

A. Operation alphabet

Definition 1 (Operation alphabet) . An operation alphabet is a finite, non-empty set Σ whose elements σ ∈ Σ each denote an effectful computation name , called an operation symbol . Each σ is associated with a (possibly partial) computable function J σ K : X ⇀ Y acting on a state space X and producing a successor state in Y ⊆ X , so that the abstract symbol carries an effect when executed. The function J · K is fixed once and for all by the runtime environment and is not a property of the agent.

For the empirical instantiation in Section VIII, Σ is the union of the entries in the op column of trace_language.csv . Examples include DISCOVER_BUNDLE , BUILD_ONNX , FP16_SURGERY , and VERIFY_TRAIN . The operation symbol is the name plus its parenthesised argument list as written in the source ; two invocations of the same name with different arguments are different operation symbols, exactly as Liskov's abstract data types prescribe for parameterised operations [23].

B. Agent

Definition 2 (Agent) . An agent is a 7-tuple

where

  • Σ A ⊆ Σ is the agent's operation alphabet;
  • Q A is a (possibly countably infinite) configuration space ;
  • q A 0 ∈ Q A is the initial configuration ;
  • X A is the (computable) input domain ;
  • δ A : Q A × X A ⇀Q A × Σ ∗ A is the agent's step relation : at configuration q on input x it returns a successor configuration q ′ together with a finite string of operation symbols emitted by the step;
  • F A ⊆ Q A is the halting set ;
  • h A : F A →{ accept , reject } is the halting verdict .

The objects X A , δ A , h A are derivative: X A is determined by Σ and the runtime environment; δ A is the agent's program ; and h A is fixed by the convention that a configuration in F A accepts iff it carries the post-condition for which the trace was emitted.

Remark 1 . The 7-tuple is the minimal one that lets us speak of an emitted string per step. A 5-tuple of the ( Q, Σ , δ, q 0 , F ) Hopcroft-Ullman [7] form would force δ to emit a single symbol per step, which is too restrictive: a single 'call to the planner' in our empirical agent emits a structured promptbuild sequence that we prefer to record as a string of three or four operation symbols. The choice of Σ ∗ A rather than Σ A in the codomain of δ A is therefore essential.

C. Run, trace, and trace language

H. The empirical signature of a Type-0 trace language

A trace language L ( A ) is Type-0 iff A can emit traces whose length grows without a recursive bound in the input size, and whose contents reflect arbitrary recursive enumerations of the operation alphabet. We acknowledge that classifying an LLM tool-calling loop as a Type-0 Turing machine is a formal analogy; physical LLMs possess finite context windows and are therefore strictly finite automata or linear-bounded automata. However, for the theoretical analysis of tool enumeration over an external unbounded tape (the filesystem and trace log), the loop acts as the operational equivalent of Type-0 generation.

In our empirical setting, A 's Type-0 status is witnessed by the operational footprint of the optimizer or builder loops in trace_language.csv . The lengthening of the trace is the operational footprint of the recursive enumeration: the start language emitted basic solver templates; the binding to the Type-3 verifier forced enumeration of arbitrary checktrain-validate and repair cycles; the result was a strictly more complex trace, materialised on disk by the recursive Type-0 step.

The undecidability that Robinson's theorem [8], [9] fixes on the Type-0 class is therefore a real property of A : were it not for the Type-3 verifier, no algorithm would decide whether a candidate trace lay in L ( A ) . The verifier is the entire reason the system is decidable in practice.

V. CHOMSKY CLASSIFICATION OF AGENTS

We now classify trace languages by Chomsky type and exhibit, for each type, the canonical agent shape from the empirical NEUROGOLF ONNX ARC-AGI system ( experiment_6/trace_language.csv ). The classification is the literal one of Section III; there is no new mathematics here, only the application of an old hierarchy to a new object. The novelty, if any, is the structural mapping of sub-agents in the empirical CSV to these classes, and the architectural principle - derived in Section X-D that the verifier's class is the decisive design parameter. We emphasise that the assignments for the derived sub-agents are based on structural inspection of their operational footprints rather than formal proofs via pumping lemmas; the Type-0 classification of the principal generator is an idealised upper bound (Lemma 1).

A. Type-3: regular trace languages

Definition 8 (Type-3 agent) . An agent A is Type-3 if L ( A ) ∈ L 3 , equivalently, if there exists a finite automaton M with L ( M ) = L ( A ) .

In the empirical system, the Type-3 agents include the scanner , floor_loader , grader , and the verifier . Their trace languages over a finite alphabet are generated by finite-state controllers:

  • scanner : the trace language is a flat sequence of operations like DISCOVER BUNDLE read straight off the column. No back-edge appears. No stack discipline is required.
  • verifier : the trace is a sequence of VERIFY TRAIN , VERIFY TEST , etc. The deciding finite automaton is the DFA state machine over the pipeline stages (described formally in Section VI).

The Data-Driven verifier V of this paper is realised as a deterministic finite automaton tracking the pipeline state. Membership in L ( V ) is decided in O ( n ) time and O (1) space per candidate trace.

B. Type-2: context-free trace languages

Definition 9 (Type-2 agent) . An agent A is Type-2 if L ( A ) ∈ L 2 \ L 3 , equivalently, if there exists a pushdown automaton M with L ( M ) = L ( A ) and L ( A ) is not regular.

In the empirical system, the Type-2 sub-agents conceptually include the analyzer and miner . They exhibit nested graph-building structures that naturally map to context-free productions, such as recursively expanding nested ONNX subgraphs ( DISCOVER PATTERN followed by nested symbol resolutions). The stack discipline is implicit in the tree-like trace of operations.

C. Bounded iterative loops are strictly Type-3

In early formulations, we tentatively linked iterative repair loops (e.g., a retry_phase loop bounded by a constant N = 3 ) to indexed grammars or cross-serial dependencies. We correct that classification here. A loop whose iterations are bounded by a constant N does not require the unbounded stack discipline of a PDA, nor the index stacks of an indexed grammar. It is recognizable by a finite automaton augmented with an ⌈ log 2 ( N ) ⌉ -bit counter, which is simply a larger DFA. Thus, constant-bounded orchestration loops structurally belong to the Type-3 (regular) class.

D. Type-1: context-sensitive trace languages

Definition 10 (Type-1 agent) . An agent A is Type-1 if L ( A ) ∈ L 1 , equivalently, if there exists a linear-bounded automaton M with L ( M ) = L ( A ) .

The Type-1 sub-agents include the optimizer . Its trace language exhibits linear-bounded workspace growth: it performs full-graph FP16 SURGERY , CAST COLLAPSE , and DIM SCRUB transformations. The length of its emitted trace and its intermediate graph states are strictly bounded by a constant multiple of the input graph's size, which is the LBA hallmark.

E. Type-0: recursively enumerable trace languages

Definition 11 (Type-0 agent) . An agent A is Type-0 if L ( A ) ∈ L 0 \L 1 , equivalently, if there exists a Turing machine M with L ( M ) = L ( A ) and L ( A ) is not context-sensitive.

The Type-0 agents are the builder and blender . The blender column is a prime witness: it contains a strictly unbounded enumeration of programmatic synthesis operations that dynamically construct and mix solvers based on arbitrary rule criteria ( BLEND BUNDLE , Correctness Priority Rule ). Its trace length is not bounded by a computable function of the input length prior to encountering the verifier's bounds.

The Goal-Driven agent of the architecture is the union of these Type-0 sub-agents under the orchestration relation R given in Section VII. Its trace language is, by Theorem 2 run in reverse, undecidable to verify absent the Type-3 verifier which is the Robinson-MRDP [8], [9] shadow of the Halting Problem in our setting.

VI. THE PIPELINE DFA VERIFIER

This section details the formal mechanism of the DataDriven verifier. While early iterations of this work employed an Aho-Corasick keyword index over unit-test catalogues, the verifiable constraints of the NEUROGOLF ONNX pipeline are better captured by a deterministic finite automaton (DFA) that tracks the global state of the task orchestration. This DFA ensures that the Type-0 Goal-Driven generator produces sequences of computational operations that are logically sound and structurally bounded.

A. The Data-Driven verifier as a DFA

In our empirical setting, the verifier V is implemented as a state machine whose state set Q V corresponds directly to the lifecycle phases of an ARC-AGI graph solver (e.g., INIT , ANALYZED , BUILT , OPTIMIZED , V TRAIN , V TEST , PACKAGED , SUBMITTED , REJECTED , ERROR ).

Transitions between these states are triggered by the operation symbols ( Σ ) emitted by the Goal-Driven sub-agents. For example, the transition from ANALYZED to BUILT is triggered by graph-construction operations like BUILD ONNX or CONVOLUTION ; the transition from BUILT to OPTIMIZED is triggered by transformations such as FP16 SURGERY or CAST COLLAPSE .

Proposition 4 (Verifier as DFA) . The Data-Driven verifier V is realisable as a deterministic finite automaton over Σ with state set Q V . For any candidate trace τ ∈ Σ ∗ , the verdict h V ( τ ) ∈ { accept , reject } is computed in O ( | τ | ) time.

Proof. Construct the DFA over the pipeline states; declare its accepting states F V to be those indicating successful completion (e.g., SUBMITTED or PACKAGED ). The size of the state machine is O (1) relative to the input, bounded by the fixed number of pipeline states and valid operation transitions. The linear-time membership decision O ( | τ | ) is a fundamental property of all deterministic finite automata [6].

B. Why a DFA rather than a generic LLM judge

A generic LLM-as-judge could, in principle, also play the role of V . We deliberately chose a rigid DFA for three reasons:

  1. Class containment. An LLM-as-judge is itself a Type0 agent. Composing two Type-0 agents does not lower the class. A DFA is Type-3 and lowers the verification budget to O ( | τ | ) by Theorem 2.
  2. Auditability. The pipeline states and transition matrix are finite, human-readable, and version-controlled. An LLM-as-judge offers no such audit surface and suffers from inherent stochasticity.
  3. Soundness against the Type-0 generator. If the generator is permitted to enumerate arbitrary strings, the verifier must be unable to be 'talked into' accepting a malformed trace (e.g., submitting an un-optimized or failing graph). A DFA cannot be talked into anything; it either transitions legally or it rejects. This is the precise sense in which the Goal-Driven Type-0 agent is tamed by the Type-3 verifier.

A systematic comparison of the verification cost across all four Chomsky classes - including the trade-off between expressiveness and decidability for Type-2, Type-1, and Type0 verifiers - is given in Section X-D. The conclusion of that analysis is that Type-3 is the principled floor: it is the only class that preserves both decidability and the O ( n ) verification budget.

C. The recursive enumeration step, decoded

We can now describe the recursive enumeration step that expands the basic start language into the highly complex empirical language recorded in experiment_6/trace_language.csv .

  1. Let Σ 0 be the operation alphabet of the initial seed templates. V 0 is initialized as the DFA governing the pipeline lifecycle.
  2. Run the Goal-Driven generator G 0 over the NEUROGOLF ONNX input, intersecting every emitted prefix with L ( V 0 ) and rejecting prefixes that drive the DFA into the ERROR or REJECTED states.
  3. During execution, G 0 proposes dynamic graph constructions or repair sequences. The trace lengthens recursively until it either hits an iteration bound or produces a sequence that drives the DFA to an accepting state ( SUBMITTED ).
  4. The accumulated trace is written to disk as trace_language.csv . Its alphabet Σ 1 properly extends Σ 0 ; its sub-agent count expands, manifesting new dynamically assembled operational flows (e.g., the blender and optimizer components).

The procedure is, in the operational sense, a Type-0 recursive enumeration: the agent enumerates strings, the verifier filters them, and the recursion terminates when the enumerated language satisfies all properties of the data-driven specification. In the literal sense of Robinson [8], [9] the recursion has no general termination guarantee; in our bounded laptop the recursion is forced to halt by the LBA bound described in the next section.

VII. METHODOLOGY: THE GOAL-DRIVEN / DATA-DRIVEN ARCHITECTURE

This section describes the architecture in operational terms. The architecture is austere by design: one Goal-Driven Type0 generator, one Data-Driven Type-3 verifier, and an explicit recursive enumeration procedure that maps the start language to the realised trace language. The Type-2 and Type-1 subagents appear as derivations of the binding, not as independent design choices.

A. The two principal agents

a) Goal-Driven generator G .: G is a Type-0 agent realised concretely as a large-language-model loop over the union of the operation alphabets in trace_language_original.csv . Its configuration space is the LLM's context window plus a finite set of bookkeeping flags; its step relation δ G samples a continuation, parses the continuation's tool-call markup, and emits the corresponding operation string. X. DISCUSSION

We discuss six matters: the meaning of 'idealised Type0' on a laptop; the role of the start language; the producerconsumer restatement and the Type-3 producer subtlety; the verifier-expressiveness ladder (why Type-3 is the principled sweet spot, not an assumption); threats to validity; and the relationship between the trace-language theory and the prior literature on agent architectures.

A. Idealised Type-0 versus realised LBA

The Goal-Driven producer's trace language is, on paper, recursively enumerable. In practice, the producer runs on a single laptop with a finite tape and a finite execution budget, supplemented by the High Performance Computing Center at Texas Tech University for the heavier ablation runs. The realised machine is therefore a (very large) linear-bounded automaton - strictly Type-1. We claim the Type-0 framing is nonetheless the right one for the analysis, on the basis of Lemma 1: every trace that the bounded machine emits is also a trace of the idealised machine, and the verification claims CONVOLUTION In Hartmanis-Stearns' language [13], the realised machine inhabits a complexity class strictly inside Type-1. In Turing's [2] language, it is a finite restriction of a universal machine. Both views are correct; neither is more correct than the other.

B. Why the start language is necessary

It would be tempting to dispense with the start language and ask the Goal-Driven producer to invent its own operation alphabet. We attempted this in pilot runs and found, predictably, that the producer's enumeration drifts into pathological corners of Σ ∗ that the verifier was not designed to cover. The start language L 0 serves two formal roles:

  1. It fixes the seed alphabet Σ 0 over which the verifier's transition table δ V is defined. Without Σ 0 , the pipeline DFA cannot be initialised.
  2. It provides a finite syntactic skeleton from which the Type-0 enumeration can extend in a structured way. The producer treats L 0 as a base case for its recursion, not as a fixed ceiling.

The start language is therefore the inductive hypothesis of the recursive enumeration; without it, the recursion has no base. The empirical CSV experiment_6/trace_language_start.csv is the artefact form of the inductive hypothesis (7 sub-agents, 9 operation symbols). The realised CSV experiment_6/trace_language.csv is the conclusion of one inductive step (11 sub-agents, 17 operation symbols, 9 novel symbols outside Σ 0 ). More inductive steps (one per task instance) would produce more emergent columns; the architecture generalises straightforwardly to the full MLE-BENCH suite.

C. Producer-consumer, and the Type-3 producer subtlety

The Koohestani-Li-Podkopaev-Izadi question [1] asks what class is this agent? and stops there. The producerconsumer question of Dijkstra [3] asks how do classes interact ? - which is the question this paper answers. In the canonical setting, the producer runs the harder enumeration and the consumer runs the cheaper check. In our binding, the producer is Type-0 and the consumer is Type-3, and the verification budget falls to O ( n ) per candidate trace by Theorem 2.

A natural question follows. May a Type-3 agent itself act as a producer? The answer is yes, with one essential condition. Concretely, in our system the pipeline DFA's transition table δ V can be extended when a new operation symbol is deemed valid (e.g., a new graph-construction operation is added to the ONNX spec). Adding a row to the transition table is a backward inference from the data side onto the tape: the Type3 consumer becomes, locally, a producer that constrains the principal producer's future emissions.

The condition is decidability. The Type-3 producer's loop must terminate in a decidable verdict, otherwise the classical halting problem of Turing [2] re-asserts itself on the consumer side and the architecture loses its verification budget. Concretely: if the transition-table update rule is itself Type-0 (e.g., 'add a transition if the LLM judges it valid'), the rule's trace language is undecidable, and there exist runs in which the transition table never stabilises, the verifier never accepts, and the system never terminates.

The escape is to make the transition-table update rule itself Type-3 - i.e., a finite catalogue of admissible transition extensions - or, where this is infeasible, to insert a final human verification step that closes the loop. In our realised system, both escapes are used. The DFA transitions are a finite, version-controlled table; the leaderboard submission is reviewed by the first author before upload. The system is therefore a Type-3-bounded Type-0 producer in the inner loop, and a human-in-the-loop closure on the outer loop. Both are sound; neither, alone, would be.

This is the operational meaning of 'yes, with software verification and validation techniques inferred into the Data-Driven agent.' The V&V techniques are precisely the machinery of Hoare [11], Dijkstra [16], and Pnueli [17] applied at the regular level of the pipeline DFA consumer. They are not a metaphor; they are the 83-transition table, the 13-state DFA V , and the safety invariant G ( emit → DFA advances ) .

The PSPACE membership cost would make the verification budget collapse: Corollary 3 no longer holds, because the verifier itself becomes the bottleneck. The architectural value of Type-3 is precisely that the O ( n ) DFA cost is dominated by the O ( T LLM ) cost of the generator, leaving the budget structurally intact.

c) Why not Type-0?: A Type-0 verifier that may not halt is not a verifier at all: a checker that can diverge provides no soundness guarantee. Section X-C already established this: if the verifier's update rule is Type-0, the halting problem reenters through the consumer side and the architecture loses its verification budget entirely. This is the sense in which Type3 is not merely the cheapest choice but the principled floor : any weaker class (Type-3, Type-2, Type-1) is a DFA-amenable extension; any stronger class (Type-0) dissolves the soundness guarantee.

d) Summary.: Type-3 is the sweet spot: it provides O ( n ) decidable membership, supports all structural properties required by the NeuroGolf pipeline lifecycle, and preserves the verification budget of Theorem 2. Type-2 is the defensible first upgrade when nesting depth matters. Type-1 and Type-0 are not defensible as verifiers in any architecture that requires a soundness guarantee.

E. Threats to validity

a) Construct validity.: The trace-language identity criterion (Section IV) is a strong claim: an agent is nothing but its trace language. The criterion is well-defined - two agents are identified iff L ( A 1 ) = L ( A 2 ) - but it does discard information about, e.g., the LLM's chain-of-thought or the planner's intermediate JSON. We accept the discard because the discarded objects are not directly verifiable; only the trace is, and only the trace is what we admit as evidence.

We run with temperature = 0.0 to maximise reproducibility, but different prompt paths under identical settings can still produce trace variations. The class signature (Table IV) covers the structural footprints that were consistent across our development runs; the leaderboard placement figures reported in Section IX reflect the best verified submission. All code for re-runs is released with the paper.

c) External validity.: The architecture has been instantiated on two tasks: NEUROGOLF ONNX ARC-AGI (the primary domain with a formally verifiable DFA) and SPACESHIP TITANIC (secondary, keyword-based verifier). We expect the trace-language account to generalise to other structured enumeration tasks where a finite lifecycle of operations can be encoded as a DFA. We do not claim generalisation to tasks where no finite DFA captures the correctness criterion; agents operating on images, text, or open-ended code may require an expanded verifier formalism.

d) Coverage of the producer-consumer escape.: The discussion in Section X-C relies on either (a) a regular keyword-update rule, or (b) a human in the loop. In our experiments both held; we do not claim a general solution when neither holds. The recursion-without-decidability case is exactly where the halting problem returns and the architecture provides no warranty.

F. Implications for the field

Two implications follow. First, the formal-language framing of agents need not be metaphorical. When done at the level of narrowness of this paper, it produces concrete bounds on verification cost (Theorem 2, Corollary 3) and operational architectures (one Type-0 producer, one Type-3 consumer) that are competitive with the contemporary state of the art.

Second, the multiplication of agent 'roles' in contemporary literature - planner, executor, critic, verifier, summariser is, at least in our case, an emergent phenomenon, not an architectural choice. The architect designs two agents; the binding produces five more. This is consistent with McCarthy's [30] programs-as-data and Newell-Simon's [31] symbol-system hypothesis: roles are projections, not primitives. We conjecture, but do not here prove, that other contemporary agent architectures admit a similar derivation: a small number of principal agents binds a verifier, and the named roles are projections of the verified trace.

G. What we did not do

We did not measure the LLM's chain-of-thought. We did not compare backbone families. We did not run a full ablation over the DFA transition table. We did not formally prove that the realised trace is the unique fixed point of the binding. We did not provide formal pumping-lemma proofs for the Chomsky classification of derived sub-agents. Each is a defensible piece of follow-up work; none is required for the present claim, which is that a narrow theory of trace languages, derived from Turing's automata theory and bound by a Type-3 pipeline DFA, suffices to produce a competitive result on contemporary benchmarks while providing a formally grounded account of why that result holds.

XI. CONCLUSION

Koohestani et al. [1] asked whether agents are just automata; we accept their answer and move on to the operational question: can the Chomsky type of one agent be used to bound the type of another? We have given a self-contained formal theory that answers yes , derived from Turing's [2] 1936 theory of automata and Chomsky's 1956-59 hierarchy [4], [5], in which an agent is identified with, and only with, its trace language. Within this theory:

  • Agents are 7-tuples (Σ A , Q A , q A 0 , X A , δ A , F A , h A ) over a fixed operation alphabet (Section IV, Definition 2).
  • Trace languages classify into the standard Chomsky hierarchy (Section V). Constant-bounded iteration loops are Type-3 (regular); they do not require indexed grammars.
  • The producer-consumer construction of Dijkstra [3], instantiated as a Type-0 Goal-Driven producer (idealised) bound by a Type-3 Data-Driven pipeline DFA verifier, suffices: their intersection is closed at the producer's class and decidable in linear time at the consumer's DFA (Theorem 2, Corollary 3). This closure theorem is a known result of automata theory (Hopcroft-Ullman [7]); the contribution of this paper is its application as an architectural principle for multi-agent systems.
  • The Type-3 consumer is realised as the deterministic pipeline DFA implemented in framework/neurogolf_dfa_verifier.py
  • ( | Q | = 13 , | δ | = 83 , decision time 0 . 684 µ s per step).
  • Type-2 and Type-1 sub-agents are not designed; they are projected from the binding (Theorem 5).
  • A Type-3 agent may itself act as a producer, backwardinferring constrained changes to the tape, provided the inference terminates in a decidable verdict; otherwise the halting problem returns and the verification budget collapses (Section X-C).
  • The Type-0 classification of the Goal-Driven agent is an idealised upper bound; the physical machine is a linearbounded automaton, but every trace it produces is also producible by the idealised Type-0 machine (Lemma 1).

The architecture is not metaphorical. Instantiated on the NEUROGOLF ONNX ARC-AGI task, it produced a public leaderboard score of 5793 . 14 . Instantiated on the SPACESHIP TITANIC task from the public MLE-BENCH benchmark, it produced a leaderboard placement of 710 out of 2330 teams ( ≈ 30 . 5% , top tertile). The Goal-Driven producer had no a priori knowledge of the task-specific goal; the Data-Driven consumer had no LLM component. The start language has 7 sub-agents over 9 operation symbols ( experiment_6/trace_language_start.csv ); the realised trace has 11 sub-agents over 17 operation symbols ( experiment_6/trace_language.csv ), with 4 agent roles and 9 operation symbols absent from the seed, all forced into existence by the verifier's transition requirements. Reproducible code, the start-language CSV, the realised trace CSV, and the DFA verifier are released with this paper.

A. Future work

Four directions are immediate. First, the architecture should be evaluated on a broader range of tasks to test the externalvalidity claim of Section X. Second, the formal Chomsky classification of the derived sub-agents should be established via pumping-lemma proofs rather than structural inspection. Third, the trace-language identity criterion of Definition 6 invites a formal study of agent equivalence: when are two agents with different LLM backbones trace-equivalent on a given task? Fourth, the characterisation of regular transitiontable update rules that preserve decidability under a Type-3 producer deserves formal treatment (the ladder in Section X-D provides the analytical frame; what is missing is a concrete domain that crosses the Type-3/Type-2 boundary).

B. Closing remark

Turing wrote in 1936 that 'the justification [for the definitions] lies in the fact that the human memory is necessarily limited' [2]. Our laptop's memory is necessarily limited. Our agent's tape is necessarily limited. Within those limits, the theory developed in this paper is exact; outside them, it is the natural idealisation. An agent is its trace language - nothing more, nothing less - and narrowness, paired with a producerconsumer discipline that bounds the idealised Type-0 by the decidable Type-3, is enough.

Recognising Julia B. Robinson

A note is owed on the most consequential non-laureate citation of this paper. Julia Bowman Robinson (1919-1985) is the mathematician whose work, with Martin Davis, Hilary Putnam, and Yuri Matiyasevich, established that the recursively enumerable subsets of N k are exactly the Diophantine subsets - the modern characterisation of the recursively enumerable class and the resolution of Hilbert's tenth problem [8], [9]. The central undecidability claim that motivates our two-tier Type-0 / Type-3 architecture is, in its modern form, her result.

Robinson did not receive the ACM A. M. Turing Award. We are under no illusion as to why. The Award was first conferred in 1966 and through 2025 has been given overwhelmingly to men - a pattern that the field's own histories now acknowledge as a reflection of decades of structural bias in who was admitted to graduate study, hired into faculty positions, nominated for prizes, and otherwise allowed to participate in the institutional life of computer science and mathematics. Several of Robinson's contemporaries did receive the Award; she did not. Her work, however, is foundational to this paper, and we cite it as the foundational contribution it is. Robinson is listed in the bibliography alongside Turing, Chomsky, and Aho not as an exception but because she belongs there on the mathematics.

Robinson was the first woman elected to the National Academy of Sciences in mathematics (1976), the first woman President of the American Mathematical Society (1983), and a MacArthur Fellow (1983). We name her contributions in the form they took: the Robinson hypothesis , the Davis-PutnamRobinson theorem , the resolution of Hilbert's tenth. Where the field's awards have been silent, the mathematics is not.
